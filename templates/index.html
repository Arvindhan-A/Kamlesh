Chapter 01 · The Origin Point

From Class 11 CS Lab to Self-Hosting Wizardry 💻

In June 2025, we met and sat together in Hema TS ma'am's Class 11 Computer Science class. Back then, coding was brand new territory for you—you were asking how the syntax worked, debugging basic logic errors, and trying to wrap your head around programming fundamentals. I was glad to teach and guide you through those first concepts.

Fast forward to today: you literally self-host your own home servers, configure networking clusters, spin up isolated homelabs, and debug infrastructure that would make most college students sweat. Watching that evolution from asking basic questions in Class 11 to becoming a hands-on tech architect has been one of the most rewarding things to witness.

Chapter 02 · Late Night Operations

Hours of Calls & Midnight Syllabus Crunches 📞

Every single memorable phone call started with the exact same sentence: “Bro, quick 2-minute syllabus doubt.”

Two and a half hours later, neither of us had opened the textbook. We were deep into debates about server operating systems, dissecting life philosophies, laughing until our stomachs hurt, or panicking about the exam in 5 hours. Those late-night study crunches and endless calls turned school stress into core memories.

Chapter 03 · Rare Athletic Endeavors

The Rare Badminton Matches (Pure Chaos) 🏸

We didn't play badminton often—it was a rare occasion occurrence. But whenever we picked up those racquets, it was never about who won the match. It was 10% athleticism, 90% wild swings, chasing shuttlecocks into random corners, and laughing so hard we could barely serve. Every rare match was legendary.

One Last Thing.

A personal, unfiltered letter from Arvindhan to close out our school era.

TO / RECIPIENT
Kamleshvar · The Driver & Self-Hosting Wizard 🛵💻
FROM / SENDER

ERA & DATE
June 2025 to Class 12 Finale · 2026 ⏳

Kamleshvar,

If you told me back in June 2025 when we were sitting in Hema TS ma'am's CS class that I'd one day build an entire website dedicated to our friendship, I probably would have laughed.

Back then, you were just starting out with CS—asking questions, trying to wrap your head around how code worked, and figuring out the basics. I helped you take those first few steps. And watching you evolve from that beginner into someone who today literally self-hosts server clusters, builds homelabs, and solves complex tech setups on your own... that's easily one of the coolest things I've witnessed.

Then came the late-night calls. How many times did one of our phone calls start with: “Bro, just a quick 2-minute syllabus question...” and then suddenly it's 2:30 AM, and we're talking about server architectures, complaining about exams, debating wild life theories, or just laughing at something utterly stupid? Those midnight study sessions weren't just exam prep; they were where our brotherhood got locked in.

And of course, the rides. Whether it was riding our cycles at 7:00 AM on Marina Beach on our very first official hangout, or cruising through Chennai traffic on the bike and scooty. You were always the designated driver handling every single turn, while I sat behind contributing zero navigation and 100% moral support. I wouldn't trade that passenger seat for anything in the world.

And now, here we are. Class 12 is coming to an end. It's bittersweet and a little surreal knowing there are only a few moments left of these school days. From organizing NextGen 26'1 together to late-night study crunches, we've built memories that will last a lifetime. Soon we'll both be moving on to colleges, new chapters, and different roads. But school life ending doesn't change a single thing between us. The late-night calls will keep happening. The tech debates won't stop. The rare badminton matches will still be chaotic. And whenever we're on a ride, you're still going to be the driver.

“You spent years in the driver’s seat steering through every road and midnight turn we ever took—while I sat behind contributing zero GPS and 100% moral support. Consider this archive Arvindhan’s way of finally taking the wheel. Happy Birthday, Kamleshvar!”

Thank you for being genuine, unbiased, kind, and an irreplaceable brother.

Your permanent passenger & brother forever,
Arvindhan
P.S. — Self-hosting is cool, but seriously, get some sleep before exams. 😉
